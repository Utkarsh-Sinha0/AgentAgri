# 1. INTRODUCTION & GLOBAL IMPORTANCE

## 1.1 The Staple of Humanity

Rice (*Oryza sativa* L.) is the primary food source for more than half of the world's population. It provides 20% of global calorie intake and is cultivated in over 100 countries across all continents except Antarctica. Global rice production exceeds 780 million tonnes annually from approximately 167 million hectares.

### Key Statistics
- **Global production**: ~780 Mt/year (milled rice equivalent)
- **Area under cultivation**: ~167 million ha
- **Major producers**: China, India, Indonesia, Bangladesh, Vietnam, Thailand, Myanmar, Philippines, Pakistan, Brazil
- **Average yield**: ~4.7 t/ha (worldwide); ~2.7 t/ha (India); ~6.9 t/ha (China)
- **Top export nations**: India, Thailand, Vietnam, Pakistan, USA
- **Rice provides**: 20% of global per capita energy and 15% of protein

### Rice Classification by Grain Type
| Type | Grain Length | Examples | Aroma | Cooking Characteristics |
|------|-------------|----------|-------|------------------------|
| Long-grain | 6-8 mm, slender | Basmati, Jasmine | Aromatic (Basmati) | Fluffy, separate grains |
| Medium-grain | 5-6 mm, slightly round | Arborio, Calrose | Mild | Creamy, holds shape |
| Short-grain | 4-5 mm, round | Sushi rice, Glutinous | Subtle | Sticky, clumps together |
| Aromatic | Varies | Basmati, Jasmine, Thai fragrant | Strong aroma | Distinct flavor profile |
| Glutinous (Waxy) | Short-medium | Sticky rice | None | Very sticky; used in desserts |
| Pigmented | Varies | Black rice, Red rice | Nutty | High anthocyanin content |

### Rice Ecosystems (Global Classification)
| Ecosystem | % of Global Area | Water Source | Typical Yield |
|-----------|-----------------|--------------|---------------|
| Irrigated lowland | 55% | Controlled irrigation | 5-8 t/ha |
| Rainfed lowland | 25% | Monsoon rainfall | 2-4 t/ha |
| Rainfed upland | 12% | Direct rainfall only | 1-2 t/ha |
| Deepwater/floating | 6% | Flood waters up to 4m | 1-1.5 t/ha |
| Tidal wetlands | 2% | Tidal irrigation | 2-3 t/ha |

## 2.2 Rice Plant Anatomy

### Root System
- **Fibrous root system** (no taproot)
- **Seminal roots**: Develop from radicle after germination; function for 2-3 weeks
- **Adventitious (crown) roots**: Develop from nodes below ground; primary root system
- **Root hairs**: Dense near tips; absorb water and nutrients
- **Aerenchyma tissue**: Air spaces in roots that allow oxygen transport to submerged roots
- **Rooting depth**: 15-40 cm typically; up to 100 cm in well-drained soils

### Stem (Culm)
- Hollow internodes separated by solid nodes
- Height: 0.5-2.0 m depending on variety and conditions
- **Tillers**: Secondary shoots arising from basal nodes
  - Productive tillers: Bear panicles
  - Unproductive tillers: Do not bear panicles
- **Internode elongation**: Begins at panicle initiation

### Leaves
- Alternate arrangement on culm
- Components: Leaf sheath + leaf blade + ligule + auricles
- **Flag leaf**: Uppermost leaf below panicle; critical for grain filling
- **Leaf Color Chart (LCC)** used for N management: Ranges from yellow-green (1) to dark green (6)

### Panicle (Inflorescence)
- Terminal, branched raceme
- **Primary branches**: 8-15 per panicle
- **Secondary branches**: 6-12 per primary branch
- **Spikelets**: Each contains one floret (one grain per spikelet)
- **Spikelet number**: 60-250 per panicle
- **Grain**: Caryopsis (fruit where pericarp fused to seed coat)

### Rice Grain Structure
| Component | % of Brown Rice | Composition |
|-----------|----------------|-------------|
| Husk/hull | 18-20% (of paddy) | Cellulose, lignin, silica |
| Bran layer | 5-8% | Fiber, oil, protein, vitamins |
| Endosperm | 89-92% | Starch (80-85%), protein (7-10%) |
| Germ/embryo | 2-3% | Oil, protein, vitamins |

## 2.3 Growth Stages (Complete Development)

Rice development is divided into three main phases spanning 90-200+ days depending on variety.

### Phase 1: Vegetative Stage (Days 0-55)

| Stage | Duration | Key Events | Management Focus |
|-------|----------|------------|------------------|
| **0. Seed germination** | 0-3 DAS | Seed imbibes water (25-30% moisture); radicle emerges | Good seedbed; adequate moisture |
| **1. Seedling emergence** | 3-7 DAS | Coleoptile breaks soil; first leaf emerges | Shallow water (1-2 cm); bird control |
| **2. Early vegetative** | 7-21 DAS | 2-4 leaves; seminal root system established | Nitrogen starter; shallow water |
| **3. Active tillering** | 21-45 DAT | Tiller emergence (main shoot + tillers); root system expands | Water management; N top-dressing; weed control |
| **4. Maximum tillering** | 45-55 DAT | Peak tiller number; some tillers die back | Drainage for pause; N management |

Note: DAT = Days After Transplanting; DAS = Days After Sowing

### Phase 2: Reproductive Stage (Days 55-90)

| Stage | Duration | Key Events | Management Focus |
|-------|----------|------------|------------------|
| **5. Panicle initiation** | 55-65 DAT | First sign: internode elongation (1-2 cm); panicle primordia visible | Maintain 5 cm water; K application; pest scouting |
| **6. Panicle development** | 65-80 DAT | Panicle grows inside flag leaf sheath (10-12 days before heading) | Avoid stress; manage water |
| **7. Booting** | 80-85 DAT | Panicle swells within flag leaf sheath | Maintain water; blast fungicide if needed |
| **8. Heading** | 85-90 DAT | Panicle emerges from flag leaf sheath | Avoid nitrogen; protect from birds |
| **9. Flowering/anthesis** | 90-95 DAT | Spikelets open 1-2 hours; pollination occurs | Optimal temperature 25-35°C; avoid rain during flowering |

### Phase 3: Ripening Phase (Days 95-130+)

| Stage | Duration | Key Events | Management Focus |
|-------|----------|------------|------------------|
| **10. Milk stage** | 95-105 DAT | Grain fills with milky liquid (white); starch accumulation begins | Maintain water; avoid stress |
| **11. Dough stage** | 105-120 DAT | Grain consistency changes from soft to hard dough | Drain water 10-14 days before harvest |
| **12. Maturity** | 120-130+ DAT | Grain hardens; 20-25% moisture; hull turns golden brown | Harvest timing critical |

### Thermal Time Requirements (GDD = Growing Degree Days)
| Stage | Temperature Range (°C) | GDD Accumulation |
|-------|----------------------|------------------|
| Seed germination | 25-35 (opt. 30) | 50-70 |
| Seedling growth | 22-32 | 150-200 |
| Active tillering | 25-32 | 350-450 |
| Panicle initiation | 22-30 | 200-300 |
| Booting/Heading | 25-32 | 150-200 |
| Grain filling | 20-30 | 400-500 |
| **Total (120-day variety)** | | **~1300-1800 GDD** |

# 3. CLIMATE & SOIL REQUIREMENTS

## 3.1 Climate Requirements

| Factor | Optimal Range | Critical Limits |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| Temperature (germination) | 25-35°C | Min 10°C; Max 45°C |
| Temperature (vegetative) | 25-32°C day / 18-24°C night | Below 15°C slows growth; above 38°C causes stress |
| Temperature (flowering) | 25-35°C | Below 20°C: spikelet sterility; Above 38°C: high sterility |
| Temperature (grain filling) | 22-28°C day / 18-22°C night | High temps reduce grain weight and quality |
| Humidity | 70-85% (flowering); 60-70% (ripening) | High humidity increases disease; low humidity increases water demand |
| Solar radiation | 300-500 cal/cm²/day | Low radiation = poor tillering and yield |
| Rainfall (irrigated) | 800-1200 mm/season | Well-distributed |
| Rainfall (rainfed) | 1200-2500 mm/season | Must match crop water demand |
| Wind | Calm to light breeze | Strong wind at flowering causes sterility; lodging |

## 3.2 Soil Requirements

| Factor | Optimal | Tolerable Range |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| Soil texture | Clay loam, silty clay loam | Sandy to heavy clay |
| Soil depth | >25 cm | Minimum 15 cm |
| pH | 5.5-6.5 (slightly acidic) | 4.5-8.0 |
| Organic matter | >1.5% | >0.5% minimum |
| CEC (cation exchange capacity) | >15 meq/100g | Higher is better for nutrient retention |
| EC (salinity) | <2 dS/m | Tolerates up to 3-4 dS/m (some varieties up to 8-10 dS/m) |
| Available N | >250 kg/ha | Highly responsive to N fertilizer |
| Available P₂O₅ | >25 kg/ha | Low P common in acid soils |
| Available K₂O | >150 kg/ha | Often sufficient in clay soils |

### Soil Suitability Classification for Rice
| Class | Description | Characteristics |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| Class I (Highly suitable) | Deep clay loam, good water-holding, pH 5.5-7.0, level land | Yields >5 t/ha with good management |
| Class II (Moderately suitable) | Medium-textured, slight slope, moderate water-holding | Yields 3-5 t/ha |
| Class III (Marginally suitable) | Sandy, shallow, saline, acidic, undulating | Yields 1.5-3 t/ha |
| Class IV (Not suitable) | Gravelly, highly saline, very shallow, steep slopes | Not recommended |

### Soil Problems in Rice Fields
| Problem | Cause | Management |
|---------|-------|------------|
| Iron toxicity | Low pH, poor drainage, reduced soil conditions | Drainage; lime; resistant varieties |
| Salinity/Alkalinity | High salts in soil/irrigation water | Leaching; gypsum; salt-tolerant varieties (CSR series) |
| Zinc deficiency | High pH, high P, low organic matter | Zinc sulfate application (25-50 kg/ha); Zn-enriched urea |
| Sulfur deficiency | Continuous cropping with S-free fertilizers | Single superphosphate; gypsum; S-containing fertilizers |
| Phosphorus fixation | Highly acidic or calcareous soils | Rock phosphate (acid soils); SSP; band placement |

# 5. LAND PREPARATION

## 5.1 Pre-Planting Operations

| Operation | Purpose | Timing |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| **Summer/deep plowing** (Moldboard plow 20-25 cm depth) | Expose pests to predators and sun; improve percolation; incorporate residues | 1-2 months before planting (May-June in India) |
| **Harrowing (2-3 passes)** | Break clods; level soil; incorporate weeds | Before puddling |
| **Puddling** (wet tillage with 5-7 cm water) | Create soft seedbed; reduce percolation; control weeds; facilitate transplanting | Immediately before transplanting |
| **Land leveling** (laser-guided preferred) | Uniform water depth; better weed control; even crop maturity | After puddling |
| **Bund preparation** (raised boundaries 30-45 cm) | Retain water; prevent cross-contamination | During land prep |
| **Drain/irrigation channel maintenance** | Water control; drainage | Before planting |

## 5.2 Puddling — The Critical Operation

### Objectives of Puddling
1. Soften soil for easy transplanting
2. Reduce water percolation losses (create hard pan at 15-25 cm depth)
3. Incorporate weeds and crop residues
4. Create anaerobic conditions that suppress weed seed germination
5. Reduce soil volume, increase bulk density

### Puddling Intensity
| Level | Operations | Effect |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Light | 1-2 passes with cultivator in water | Minimal hard pan; moderate weed control |
| Medium | 2-3 passes + planking | Good hard pan; good weed control |
| Intensive | 3-4 passes + repeated planking | Excellent hard pan; excellent weed control |

### Water Depth During Puddling
- Too deep (>7 cm): Poor soil mixing, implements float
- Too shallow (<3 cm): Implements drag, uneven puddling
- Optimal: 5-7 cm standing water

## 5.3 Zero-Tillage / Minimum Tillage Options

For direct-seeded rice (DSR), puddling is avoided:

| Method | Operations | Suitable for |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Zero-till DSR | Direct drilling into untilled soil | Where previous crop residue retained |
| Reduced till DSR | 1-2 harrowings + seeding | Medium-textured soils |
| Strip tillage | Till only seed row | Conservation agriculture systems |
| Bed planting | Form raised beds, sow on beds | Heavy soils with drainage issues |

# 6. CULTIVATION METHODS

## 6.1 Transplanted Rice (TPR) — Conventional Method

### Advantages
- Best weed control (puddling + flooded conditions)
- Uniform crop stand
- Higher yield potential
- Efficient water use (shorter field duration)
- Nitrogen-efficient (ammonium form available in flooded soil)

### Disadvantages
- High labor requirement (25-35 person-days/ha)
- High nursery area requirement (1/10 of main field)
- More water required for land preparation (puddling)
- Higher seedling production costs
- Transplanting shock (7-10 days recovery)

## 6.2 Direct-Seeded Rice (DSR)

### Types of DSR
| Type | Description | Water Management | Suitable For |
|------|-------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Dry DSR | Sowing dry seed into dry soil (like wheat) | Rainfed/irrigated as needed | Uplands, rainfed areas |
| Wet DSR | Sowing pre-germinated seed onto puddled soil | Flooded after seedling establishment | Irrigated lowlands |
| Aerobic rice | Sowing dry seed into well-drained soil | Intermittent irrigation | Water-scarce areas |

### Advantages of DSR
- Labor saving (reduces 70-80% labor vs transplanting)
- No nursery requirement
- 25-40% less water (no puddling; reduced percolation)
- Earlier maturity (7-10 days earlier than TPR)
- Reduced methane emissions
- Better root development (no transplant shock)
- Mechanization-friendly

### Challenges in DSR
- Higher weed pressure (no puddling + no standing water initially)
- More difficult crop establishment
- Greater risk of poor stand if rains fail
- Higher seed rate required (20-30 kg/ha vs 8-10 kg/ha for TPR)
- Nitrogen management more complex (aerobic conditions → nitrate leaching)
- Greater micronutrient deficiencies possible

### Seed Rate Comparison
| Method | Seed Rate (kg/ha) | Plant Spacing |
|--------|------------------|---------------|
| Transplanted — seedlings | 8-12 | 20×15 cm to 20×20 cm |
| Dry DSR (drilling) | 20-30 | 20-25 cm rows |
| Wet DSR (broadcasting) | 40-60 | Random |
| SRI (young seedlings) | 5-7 | 25×25 cm or wider |
| DSR with drum seeder | 20-25 | 20 cm rows |
| DSR with precision seeder | 15-25 | Adjustable |

## 6.3 System of Rice Intensification (SRI)

### Core Principles
1. **Young seedlings**: Transplant at 8-12 days old (2-leaf stage), not 25-35 days
2. **Single seedling per hill**: No clumps of 3-4 seedlings
3. **Wide spacing**: 25×25 cm or wider in square pattern
4. **Alternate wetting and drying (AWD)**: No continuous flooding
5. **Organic soil enrichment**: Compost, FYM, biofertilizers
6. **Mechanical weeding**: Cono-weeder between rows (aerates soil)

### SRI Benefits (Research-validated in 60+ countries)
| Parameter | Conventional | SRI | Improvement |
|-----------|-------------|-----|-------------|
| Yield (t/ha) | 4-6 | 6-10 | 20-100% |
| Seed required (kg/ha) | 40-60 | 5-7 | 85-90% reduction |
| Water use (mm) | 1200-2000 | 600-1000 | 25-50% reduction |
| Methane emissions | High | 40-60% lower | Significant reduction |
| Input costs | Higher | 20-30% lower | Economic benefit |

### SRI Spacing Recommendations
| Spacing | Plants/m² | Suitable For |
|---------|-----------|--------------|
| 25×25 cm | 16 | Most varieties, moderate fertility |
| 30×30 cm | 11 | High fertility, high-tillering varieties |
| 40×40 cm | 6 | Very fertile soils, long-duration varieties |
| 25×20 cm | 20 | Medium fertility, moderate tillering |

## 6.4 Hybrid Rice Cultivation

### Seed Production
- Hybrid rice exploits heterosis (hybrid vigor)
- 15-30% yield advantage over inbred varieties
- Requires parental line multiplication (A line, B line, R line)
- Seed production requires isolation (100-200 m from other rice)
- Synchronized flowering of A and R lines critical
- Seed yield: 1.5-2.5 t/ha (hybrid seed)

### Hybrid Cultivation Management
| Factor | Recommendation |
|--------|---------------|
| Seed rate | 10-15 kg/ha (transplanted); 12-18 kg/ha (DSR) |
| Seedling age | 18-22 days (slightly older than inbred) |
| Plant spacing | 20×15 cm or 20×20 cm |
| Nitrogen | 20-30% more than inbreds (150-200 kg N/ha) |
| Water management | Continuous flooding preferred |
| Panicles/m² | 300-400 target |

## 6.5 Transplanting Methods Compared

| Method | Labor (person-days/ha) | Cost (relative) | Plant Establishment | Suitable Scale |
|--------|----------------------|-----------------|---------------------|----------------|
| Manual transplanting | 25-35 | Medium | Good | Small farms |
| Mechanical transplanter (walk-behind) | 5-8 | High initial | Good | Medium farms |
| Mechanical transplanter (riding) | 3-5 | Very high initial | Excellent | Large farms |
| Parachute method (seedlings on trays, broadcast) | 8-12 | Medium | Variable | Any scale |
| Drum seeder (wet DSR) | 3-5 | Low | Good in puddled soil | Medium-large |
| Droni (manual seeding tube) | 10-12 | Very low | Moderate | Small farms |

# 7. NURSERY MANAGEMENT & TRANSPLANTING

## 7.1 Nursery Types

|-------------|--------------------------------|------------------------|---------------------|----------|
| **Wet-bed nursery** | 1:10 | 25-30 | 20-30 | Irrigated lowland |
| **Dry-bed nursery** | 1:10 | 30-40 | 20-25 | Upland/rainfed |
| **Modified mat nursery** | 1:40 | 8-10 | 12-15 | SRI |
| **Dapog nursery** (banana leaves/polyethylene) | 1:100 | 8-10 | 10-12 | Mechanical transplanting |
| **Ebb-tray nursery** | 1:80-100 | 8-10 | 10-14 | For mechanical transplanting |
| **Paddy-on-tray** | 1:80 | 6-8 | 8-12 | SRI, mechanical |

## 7.2 Wet-Bed Nursery Management (Standard Method)

### Bed Preparation
1. Select level, well-drained area near water source
2. Pudddle soil thoroughly with 5 cm water
3. Form raised beds: 1-1.2m wide, convenient length (15-20 m)
4. Beds 10-15 cm above ground level
5. Apply FYM/compost 5-10 t/ha (nursery area)
6. Apply N:P₂O₅:K₂O at 50:50:50 kg/ha (nursery area)

### Seed Treatment
| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| 1. **Seed selection** | Use certified/truthfully labeled seed; 8-10 hours sun drying |
| 2. **Floatation test** | Discard floaters in salt solution (1.04 specific gravity = 1 egg floating in water) |
| 3. **Hot water treatment** | 52°C for 10 minutes (controls seed-borne blast) |
| 4. **Fungicide treatment** | Carbendazim 2g/kg seed OR Carboxin + Thiram 2g/kg OR Trichoderma 4g/kg |
| 5. **Soaking** | 12-24 hours in clean water |
| 6. **Incubation** | Drain water; incubate in gunny bags for 24-48 hours until radicle appears |
| 7. **Pre-germinated for wet DSR** | Continue incubation until radicle and plumule 1-2 mm |

### Nursery Sowing
- Sow pre-germinated seed uniformly on bed surface
- Cover with thin layer of fine soil/FYM
- Maintain thin water layer (1-2 cm) in nursery channels (not on beds)
- Top-dress with 20 kg N/ha as urea at 15 days after sowing

### Seedling Management
| Parameter | Conventional | SRI/Mechanical |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| Sowing density (g/m² nursery) | 50-80 | 10-15 |
| Seedling height at transplanting (cm) | 15-25 | 8-15 |
| Leaves at transplanting | 4-5 | 2-3 |
| Age at transplanting (days) | 20-35 | 8-15 |

## 7.3 Transplanting Operations

### Age of Seedlings by System
| System | Seedling Age (days) | Leaves | Reason |
|--------|---------------------|--------|--------|
| Conventional TPR (cool season) | 25-35 | 4-5 | Slow growth in low temps |
| Conventional TPR (warm season) | 20-25 | 4-5 | Standard practice |

- Too deep (5+ cm): Reduced tillering, poor establishment
- Too shallow (<1 cm): Seedlings float, poor anchoring

| SRI | 1 | Maximize individual plant tillering potential |
| Hybrid rice | 1-2 | Higher seed cost; hybrid vigor compensates |
| Cold areas | 4-5 | Compensate for slow growth |
| Saline areas | 4-5 | Compensate for poor establishment |

# 8. WATER MANAGEMENT & IRRIGATION

## 8.1 Water Requirements of Rice

Rice is the most water-intensive cereal crop, requiring 3,000-5,000 liters of water per kg of grain produced (including field evaporation and percolation).

### Water Balance in Paddy Fields
| Component | Typical Value (mm/season) |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| Land preparation (puddling + soaking) | 150-300 |
| Evapotranspiration (ET) | 500-700 |
| Percolation (depends on soil) | 200-800 |
| Seepage (lateral movement) | 100-400 |
| **Total water requirement** | **950-2200** |
| Effective rainfall | Variable (0-1000+) |
| Net irrigation requirement | Total - Effective rainfall |

### Crop Stage-wise Water Requirement (120-day variety)
| Stage | Duration (days) | Water Depth (cm) | Days Between Irrigation |
|-------|-----------------|------------------|------------------------|
| Nursery | 25-30 | 2-3 (channel) | 1-2 |
| Land preparation | 10-15 | 5-7 standing | — |
| Transplanting-20 DAT | 20 | 3-5 standing | 2-5 |
| 20-50 DAT | 30 | 5-7 standing | 3-7 |
| 50-70 DAT (PI to heading) | 20 | 5-7 standing | 2-5 |
| Flowering (5-7 days) | 7 | 5-10 standing | 2-3 |
| Grain filling | 30 | 3-5 intermittent | 5-7 |
| Ripening — 10 days before harvest | 15 | Drain field | — |

## 8.2 Water Management Methods

### Continuous Flooding (Traditional)
- **Method**: Maintain 5-10 cm standing water throughout crop cycle
- **Advantages**: Suppresses weeds; maintains anaerobic conditions for N retention; stable yields
- **Disadvantages**: Highest water use; highest methane emissions; iron reduction in soil
- **Water use**: 1500-2500 mm per season

### Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD)
- **Method**: Allow water to recede naturally; irrigate when water level drops to 10-15 cm below soil surface

- **AWD Tubes**: Perforated PVC tubes (30 cm long, 10 cm diameter) placed in field to monitor water table
- **Water saving**: 15-35% compared to continuous flooding
- **Methane reduction**: 30-70%
- **No yield loss**: If water is reapplied before soil cracks (safe AWD)
- **Sensitive period**: Do NOT practice AWD during flowering (15 days before to 10 days after heading)

### Saturation Screening
- **Method**: Keep soil saturated but no standing water (except during flowering)
- **Water saving**: 25-50%
- **Best for**: Heavy, poorly draining soils

### Mid-Season Drainage
- **Method**: Drain field completely for 7-10 days at maximum tillering
- **Purpose**: 
  - Controls unproductive tillers
  - Reduces iron toxicity
  - Allows oxygen to soil
  - Suppresses pest buildup
  - Prevents lodging
- **Timing**: 40-50 DAT (just before panicle initiation)

### Aerobic Rice System
- **Method**: Rice grown like wheat/maize with intermittent irrigation
- **Water use**: 500-900 mm
- **Best for**: Water-scarce regions
- **Challenges**: Weed pressure higher; N management more difficult; yield 20-30% less than flooded

## 8.3 Irrigation Systems for Rice

| System | Water Use Efficiency | Suitability | Cost |
|--------|---------------------|-------------|------|
| Flood irrigation (basin) | 40-60% | Level fields, traditional | Low |
| Border strip irrigation | 50-70% | Slightly sloped fields | Low |
| Furrow irrigation (raised beds) | 55-75% | Bed-planted rice | Medium |
| Sprinkler irrigation | 60-80% | Aerobic/upland rice | Medium-high |
| Drip irrigation | 80-95% | Aerobic rice, high-value | High |
| Subsurface drip | 85-95% | Permanent beds | Very high |

## 8.4 Rainfed Rice Water Management

| Water Regime | Typical Region | Coping Strategies |
|-------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Drought-prone** (early or late season) | Central India, Africa | Drought-tolerant varieties; supplemental irrigation from ponds; direct seeding |
| **Submergence-prone** (mid-season flooding) | Eastern India, Bangladesh, Mekong | Submergence-tolerant varieties (SUB1); deepwater varieties; early planting |
| **Flash flood** (sudden, brief flooding) | Flood plains | SUB1 varieties; staggered planting; seedling recovery management |
| **Deepwater** (prolonged >50cm) | Delta regions; Assam; Cambodia | Floating varieties; late planting |
| **Tidal** (coastal) | Mangrove deltas | Salt-tolerant varieties; raised beds; tidal gates |

## 8.5 Drainage Management

Good drainage is essential even for lowland rice. Poor drainage leads to:
- Iron toxicity
- Sulfide toxicity (hydrogen sulfide)
- Alcohol accumulation in roots
- Reduced root respiration
- Increased disease incidence (sheath rot, stem rot)

### Drainage Systems
| Type | Description | Spacing |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Surface drainage | Field channels, bund gaps | 20-50 m |
| Subsurface drainage (tile/pipes) | BURIED perforated pipes | 10-30 m (clay) to 30-60 m (sandy) |
| Open field drains | Peripheral channels | Field boundaries |

### Leaf Color Chart (LCC) Based N Management
- LCC reading ranges: 1 (pale yellow-green) to 6 (dark green)
- Threshold: Apply N when reading ≤ 4 (for lowland rice) or ≤ 3 (for aerobic rice)

- Timing: Weekly readings from 20 DAT to flowering

- Based on:
  - Target yield

## 10.2 Weed Management Strategies

### Cultural Control
| Practice | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| **Puddling** | Suppresses 60-80% of weed seed germination |
| **Standing water (5 cm)** | Suppresses most weeds except sedges and some broadleaves |

| **Higher seed rate / closer spacing** | Faster canopy closure; competitive suppression |
| **Clean seed** | Prevents introduction of weedy rice and Echinochloa |
| **Stale seedbed technique** | Prepare seedbed, allow weeds to emerge, kill before planting |
| **Field sanitation** | Clean bunds and channels; prevent weed seed migration |
| **Laser land leveling** | Uniform water depth = uniform weed suppression |

### Integrated Weed Management (IWM) Schedule for Rice

| Time | Action | Method |
|------|--------|--------|
| **Before planting** | Stale seedbed OR summer plowing | Non-selective herbicide OR tillage |
| **0-3 DAT** | Pre-emergence herbicide | Pretilachlor/Butachlor/Pyrazosulfuron |
| **15-25 DAT** | 1st post-emergence (if needed) | Bispyribac or Fenoxaprop |
| **20-30 DAT** | Manual weeding OR cono-weeding | Remove escaped weeds OR between-row cultivation |
| **30-40 DAT** | 2nd post-emergence (if needed) | 2,4-D for broadleaves/sedges |
| **Before canopy closes** | Ensure full canopy cover | Competition from crop itself |

### Herbicide Resistance Management
- Rotate herbicides with different modes of action (WSSA/HRAC groups)
- Use pre-emergence + post-emergence sequence (not same group twice)
- Do not apply same herbicide >2 seasons in a row
- Integrate mechanical/cultural methods
- For known resistant weeds (e.g., *Echinochloa* resistant to bispyribac), switch to MOA Group 1 (fenoxaprop) or Group 15 (pretilachlor)

# 11. MAJOR RICE DISEASES

## 11.1 Disease Overview

Rice is vulnerable to over 70 diseases, of which about 15 cause economically significant damage. Global yield loss to diseases is estimated at 10-15% annually.

| Disease Type | Number of Major Diseases | Estimated Annual Loss |
|-------------|------------------------|----------------------|
| Fungal | 10+ | 8-12% |
| Bacterial | 3 | 2-5% |
| Viral | 5+ | 1-3% |
| Nematode | 3 | <1% |

## 11.2 Fungal Diseases

### 1. Rice Blast (Magnaporthe grisea / Pyricularia oryzae)
**Most destructive rice disease worldwide** — can cause up to 100% loss in severe epidemics.

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Pathogen** | *Magnaporthe grisea* (anamorph: *Pyricularia oryzae*) |
| **Favorable conditions** | Cool temps (24-28°C); high humidity (>90%); prolonged leaf wetness; high N fertilizer; cloudy/rainy weather |
| **Survival** | On seed, crop residue, weed hosts; conidia spread by wind |

**Symptoms by Plant Part:**

| Plant Part | Symptom Description | Impact |
|-----------|---------------------|--------|
| **Leaf blast** | Diamond/elliptical lesions with gray centers and dark brown margins; lesions coalesce in severe cases | Reduced photosynthetic area; leaf death |
| **Node blast** | Black, rotten node; breaks above the infected node | Panicle dies above infection |
| **Neck blast** | Gray-brown lesion at panicle base; panicle turns white/grey | Complete grain loss in infected panicles |
| **Panicle blast** | Lesions on branches of panicle | Partial or complete sterility |
| **Seedling blast** | Seedlings killed before or after emergence | Poor stand establishment |

**Management of Rice Blast:**

| Method | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Resistant varieties** | Most effective; but pathogen races evolve rapidly; need to rotate varieties |
| **Seed treatment** | Carbendazim 2g/kg; Carboxin + Thiram 2g/kg; Hot water 52°C/10 min |
| **Fungicide (preventive)** | Tricyclazole 0.06% (75% WP) at neck emergence; Edifenphos; Isoprothiolane; Pyroquilon |
| **Fungicide (curative)** | Azoxystrobin 0.1%; Trifloxystrobin + Tebuconazole |
| **Fungicide timing** | First spray at 5% neck emergence; second at 50% heading |
| **Cultural** | Avoid excess N; manage water; avoid dense planting; remove crop residues |
| **Biological** | *Trichoderma viride* seed treatment + soil application; *Pseudomonas fluorescens* |
| **Resistance risk** | High for strobilurins; moderate for tricyclazole; rotate MOA groups |

### 2. Sheath Blight (Rhizoctonia solani AG1-IA)
Second most important rice disease globally. Increasing with high N + dense planting.

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Pathogen** | *Rhizoctonia solani* anastomosis group AG1-IA |
| **Favorable conditions** | High humidity (96-100%); temps 30-32°C; dense canopy; excess N; continuous flooding |
| **Survival** | Sclerotia in soil and crop residue; wide host range |

**Symptoms:**
- Initially: Elliptical to irregular green-gray lesions on leaf sheaths near water line
- Lesions enlarge, coalesce, center turns gray-white with brown border
- Lesions spread to leaf blades
- In severe cases: infection reaches flag leaf and panicle
- Grains may be partially filled or chaffy

**Management of Sheath Blight:**

| Method | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Resistant varieties** | No complete resistance available; some tolerance in improved varieties |
| **Cultural** | Avoid excess N; wider spacing; balanced fertilization (K helps); avoid dense planting; remove infected stubble |
| **Fungicides** | Hexaconazole 5% SC @ 1L/ha; Carbendazim 1 kg/ha; Azoxystrobin; Propiconazole; Tebuconazole; Thiophanate-methyl |
| **Timing** | At first visible symptom (30-45 DAT); repeat at 10-15 day interval |
| **Biological** | *Trichoderma harzianum*; *Pseudomonas fluorescens*; *Bacillus subtilis* |
| **Biofungicides** | Validamycin 3% L @ 2.5 L/ha |

### 3. Sheath Rot (Sarocladium oryzae)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Pathogen** | *Sarocladium oryzae* |
| **Favorable conditions** | High humidity; temps 25-30°C; rain at heading; spikelet sterility often associated |
| **Importance** | In some regions, can surpass sheath blight in severity |

**Symptoms:**
- Gray-brown lesions on uppermost leaf sheath enclosing panicle
- Sheath rots; panicle partially or completely fails to emerge
- Infected panicles have discolored, chaffy grains
- White powdery growth inside sheath in advanced stages

**Management:**
- Seed treatment: Carbendazim 2g/kg seed
- Field sanitation
- Avoid excessive N at heading
- Spray: Carbendazim 1 kg/ha at booting
- *Trichoderma* application reduces severity

### 4. Brown Spot (Cochliobolus miyabeanus / Bipolaris oryzae)
**Historical importance**: Caused the Bengal Famine of 1943 (significant contributor).

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Pathogen** | *Cochliobolus miyabeanus* (anamorph: *Bipolaris oryzae*) |
| **Favorable conditions** | Nutrient deficiency (especially K); drought stress; temps 25-30°C; high humidity |
| **Survival** | On seed, crop residue, soil |

**Symptoms:**
- **Leaves**: Small, oval, dark brown spots with gray center; numerous spots coalesce
- **Grains**: Brown/black discoloration of glumes (pecky rice)
- **Panicles**: Blackening of spikelets

**Management:**
- Balanced fertilization (especially K: 40-60 kg K₂O/ha)
- Resistant varieties
- Seed treatment: Carbendazim; hot water
- Spray: Mancozeb 2.5 g/L; Edifenphos; Propiconazole; Tebuconazole; Azoxystrobin
- *Pseudomonas fluorescens* application

### 5. Stem Rot (Magnaporthe salvinii / Sclerotium oryzae)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Pathogen** | *Magnaporthe salvinii* (anamorph: *Sclerotium oryzae*) |
| **Favorable conditions** | Continuous flooding; high N; dense planting; infected crop residue |

**Symptoms:**
- Small black lesions on outer leaf sheath near water line
- Lesions enlarge; sclerotia form inside stem
- Stems rot at base; lodging occurs
- Panicles may be unfilled or mature prematurely
- Affected plants show white panicles (like stem borer)

**Management:**
- Remove and burn infected stubble
- Avoid excess N
- Maintain proper water depth (not too deep)
- Field drainage during mid-season
- Fungicide: Carbendazim; Hexaconazole; Propiconazole

### 6. False Smut (Ustilaginoidea virens)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Pathogen** | *Ustilaginoidea virens* |
| **Favorable conditions** | High humidity at flowering; high N; cool, rainy weather |

**Symptoms:**
- Individual grains replaced by large (up to 2 cm) yellow-green balls
- Balls turn black-green with age; surface cracks
- Balls contain fungal spores (greenish-black)
- Only a few grains per panicle affected usually

**Management:**
- Avoid excess N
- Drain field before flowering
- Remove smut balls before they burst (reduce inoculum)
- Fungicide: Copper oxychloride 0.25%; Propiconazole 0.1%; at booting

### 7. Narrow Brown Leaf Spot (Cercospora oryzae / Sphaerulina oryzina)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Pathogen** | *Cercospora oryzae* |
| **Condition** | Warm, humid; late-season disease |

**Symptoms:**
- Narrow, linear, tan-brown lesions parallel to leaf veins
- Short (1-4 mm); numerous per leaf
- On leaf sheaths also
- Premature leaf senescence

**Management:**
- Moderately resistant in many varieties
- Mancozeb or carbendazim if severe

### 8. Leaf Scald (Microdochium oryzae / Rhynchosporium oryzae)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Pathogen** | *Monographella albescens* (anamorph: *Microdochium oryzae*) |
| **Condition** | High humidity; excess N; in rice-wheat systems |

**Symptoms:**
- Zig-zag pattern on leaf margins
- Water-soaked lesions at leaf tips, moving down
- Gray center with brown margins
- Premature leaf drying

**Management:**
- Balanced N
- Mancozeb; Carbendazim; Propiconazole

## 11.3 Bacterial Diseases

### 1. Bacterial Leaf Blight (Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Pathogen** | *Xanthomonas oryzae* pv. *oryzae* |
| **Favorable conditions** | High humidity; temps 28-34°C; heavy rain; wind; wounds from storms; high N |
| **Survival** | In seed, crop residue, irrigation water |
| **Yield loss** | 20-50% in severe cases |

**Symptoms:**
- **Leaf blight** (most common): Water-soaked to yellow-orange lesions starting from leaf tips/margins; lesions extend downward with wavy margins; in later stages, dried white/yellow leaves
- **Kresek** (seedling blight): Complete wilting and yellowing of seedlings (most destructive phase)
- **Pale yellow exudate**: Bacterial ooze visible as droplets on infected leaves

**Management of Bacterial Leaf Blight:**

| Method | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Resistant varieties** | Most effective; race-specific resistance (Xa genes); varieties with Xa4 + xa5 + Xa21 recommended |
| **Cultural** | Avoid high N; avoid standing water near root zone during flooding; clean seed; remove crop residue |
| **Copper sprays** | Copper oxychloride 0.25%; copper hydroxide |
| **Antibiotic** | Streptocycline (streptomycin 15% + tetracycline 1.5%) @ 3g/10L water; limited efficacy |
| **Biological** | *Pseudomonas fluorescens*; *Bacillus subtilis*; bacteriophages |
| **Seed treatment** | Hot water 52°C/30 min; streptomycin |
| **Fungicide** | Isoprothiolane + tricyclazole + streptocycline mix |

### 2. Bacterial Leaf Streak (Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzicola)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Symptoms** | Narrow, translucent, water-soaked streaks between leaf veins (1-2 mm wide); yellow exudate; lesions turn greyish |
| **Management** | Similar to BLB; seed treatment critical; copper sprays |

### 3. Sheath Brown Rot / Grain Rot (Pseudomonas glumae / P. fuscovaginae)
- Emerging disease in Asia and Americas
- Panicle discoloration; unfilled grains
- Seed treatment with mercurial compounds (banned in many countries) formerly used
- Copper-based sprays; avoid high N

## 11.4 Viral Diseases

### 1. Rice Tungro Disease (RTD)
**Most important viral disease in South and Southeast Asia**

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Pathogen** | Rice tungro bacilliform virus (RTBV) + Rice tungro spherical virus (RTSV) — vectored by green leafhopper |
| **Vector** | Green leafhopper (*Nephotettix virescens*) |
| **Favorable** | High vector populations; abundant weed hosts |

**Symptoms:**
- Stunted plants
- Yellow-orange discoloration of leaves (from tip downward)
- Reduced tillering
- Delayed flowering; incomplete panicle emergence
- Discolored (brown) grains

**Management:**
- Resistant/tolerant varieties
- Control leafhopper vectors (see pest section)
- Synchronized planting (avoid staggered planting)
- Remove infected plants (rogue)
- Eliminate weed hosts from bunds

### 2. Rice Grassy Stunt Virus (RGSV)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Vector** | Brown planthopper (*Nilaparvata lugens*) |
| **Symptoms** | Extreme stunting; excessive tillering (grass-like); yellow-green leaves with rusty spots; no or sterile panicles |

### 3. Rice Ragged Stunt Virus (RRSV)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Vector** | Brown planthopper |
| **Symptoms** | Stunting; ragged leaf margins (leaf ragged); vein swelling; delayed growth |

**Management for BPH-vectored viruses:** Control BPH (see pest section)

### 4. Rice Dwarf Virus
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Vector** | Green rice leafhopper and zigzag leafhopper |
| **Symptoms** | Dwarfing; white chlorotic spots along veins; poor panicle exsertion |

## 11.5 Nematode Diseases

| Nematode | Symptoms | Management |
|----------|----------|------------|
| **Root-knot nematode** (*Meloidogyne graminicola*) | Root galls; stunting; yellowing; yield loss 10-60% | Resistant varieties; crop rotation (non-host); bioagents (*Pasteuria penetrans*, *Trichoderma*) |
| **White tip nematode** (*Aphelenchoides besseyi*) | Leaf tip whitening (1-5 cm); reduced panicle size; increased sterility | Hot water seed treatment (53°C/15 min); clean seed |
| **Ufra nematode** (*Ditylenchus angustus*) | Leaf yellowing from base; distorted panicles | Field drying; resistant varieties |

# 12. MAJOR RICE PESTS

## 12.1 Insect Pest Overview

More than 100 insect species attack rice globally. About 20 are economically significant. Annual loss from insects is estimated at 15-25%.

| Pest Group | Examples | Damage Type | Critical Stage |
|-----------|----------|-------------|----------------|
| Stem borers | Yellow stem borer, Striped stem borer | Larvae bore stems; dead hearts + white heads | Vegetative to reproductive |
| Planthoppers | Brown planthopper, White-backed planthopper | Sap-sucking; hopperburn; virus vectors | Vegetative to heading |
| Leafhoppers | Green leafhopper | Sap-sucking; virus vectors | Vegetative growing |
| Leaf feeders | Leaf folder, Rice hispa, Caseworm | Leaf feeding; reduced photosynthesis | Vegetative |
| Grain feeders | Rice bug, Stink bug | Sucking grains; pecky rice | Grain filling |
| Root feeders | Rice water weevil, Root aphid | Root pruning; stunting | Early vegetative |
| Miscellaneous | Gall midge, Thrips, Mealybug | Shoot deformation; general stress | Early vegetative |

## 12.2 Stem Borers

### Yellow Stem Borer (Scirpophaga incertulas) — Most Important Rice Insect Pest

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Distribution** | South and Southeast Asia (monophagous — feeds only on rice) |
| **Damage symptoms** | **Dead heart**: Central leaf whorl dries and dies (vegetative stage); **White head**: Panicle emerges white and empty (reproductive stage) |
| **Life cycle** | Egg (6-8 days) → Larva (25-40 days, 5-6 instars) → Pupa (6-9 days) → Adult (3-5 days) → Total 45-60 days; 3-5 generations per season |
| **Favorable** | High N; dense planting; continuous cropping; high humidity |

**Symptoms Timeline:**
| Symptom | When to Observe | Stage Affected |
|---------|----------------|---------------|
| Egg masses on leaf tips (brown, hairy) | Early to mid-vegetative | Prevention stage |
| Dead hearts (central leaf dry, brown) | 25-50 DAT | Vegetative |
| White heads (empty, white panicles) | Heading onwards | Reproductive |
| Stem with bore holes + frass | Any stage | Confirm diagnosis |

**Management:**

| Method | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Cultural** | Remove dead hearts and white heads; clip leaf tips at transplanting (remove egg masses); summer plowing destroys pupae; avoid excess N; synchronized planting |
| **Light traps** | 1 trap/ha; monitor adult activity |
| **Pheromone traps** | 5-8 traps/ha for mass trapping AND monitoring |
| **Biological** | *Trichogramma japonicum* (egg parasitoid) — release 100,000/ha 4-5 times at weekly intervals starting 15 DAT; *Tetrastichus schoenobii* |
| **Bt sprays** | *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt kurstaki) for larval control |
| **Chemical — Granules** | Cartap hydrochloride 4G @ 25 kg/ha; Carbofuran 3G @ 33 kg/ha; Fipronil 0.3G @ 33 kg/ha |
| **Chemical — Sprays** | Chlorantraniliprole 0.4 ml/L of water; Flubendiamide; Rynaxypyr; Thiamethoxam; Novaluron |
| **Timing** | Apply when ETL is reached: 2 egg masses/m² OR 5% dead hearts |
| **ETL** (Economic Threshold Level) | Vegetative: 1 egg mass/m² (or 5% dead hearts); Reproductive: 1 moth/m² or 2 egg masses/m² |

### Striped Stem Borer (Chilo suppressalis)
- Distribution: Temperate and subtropical Asia, Europe, Middle East
- Polyphagous (attacks maize, sorghum, sugarcane also)
- Similar damage but larvae are cream-colored with dark head (VS YSB: yellow/dark brown)
- Similar management

### White Stem Borer (Scirpophaga innotata)
- Important in Indonesia and Philippines
- Similar to YSB but adapted to rainfed conditions

## 12.3 Planthoppers

### Brown Planthopper (Nilaparvata lugens) — BPH
**Most serious planthopper pest; causes hopperburn and transmits viral diseases**

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Type** | Sap-sucking (phloem feeder); uses stylet mouthparts |
| **Distribution** | Throughout Asia; tropical and subtropical |
| **Forms** | **Brachypterous** (short-winged, colonizing, high reproductive rate); **Macropterous** (long-winged, migratory) |
| **Life cycle** | Egg (7-10 days) → Nymph (5 instars, 12-18 days) → Adult (15-25 days) → Total 25-35 days; multiple overlapping generations |
| **Favorable** | High humidity; high N; close plant spacing; continuous cropping; insecticide-induced resurgence |

**Damage Types:**
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Direct feeding** | Remove phloem sap; reduce plant vigor; cause wilting and yellowing |
| **Hopperburn** | Circular patches of dried/brown plants; starts from center of field; severe = complete crop loss |
| **Honeydew** | Secretion encourages sooty mold; reduces photosynthesis |
| **Virus transmission** | Vectors rice grassy stunt (RGSV) and ragged stunt (RRSV) |

**Monitoring:**
| Method | Timing | Threshold (ETL) |
|--------|--------|-----------------|
| Visual count | Weekly, early morning | 5-10 hoppers/hill (vegetative); 10-20/hill (reproductive) |
| Sweep net | 10 sweeps/plot | >100/10 sweeps |
| Sticky trap (yellow) | Continuous | Population monitoring |
| Light trap | Nightly | Population monitoring |

**Management:**

| Method | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Resistant varieties** | Most effective; BPH-resistant varieties with BPH1-BPH38 genes; but BPH biotypes evolve rapidly |
| **Cultural** | Avoid continuous cropping (fallow period breaks cycle); avoid excess N; wide spacing; field drainage (BPH thrives in humid canopy) |
| **Conserve natural enemies** | Most important IPM strategy: avoid early-season insecticides; spiders, mirid bugs (*Cyrtorhinus lividipennis*), predatory beetles |
| **Biological** | *Cyrtorhinus lividipennis* (mirid bug — feeds on BPH eggs); *Pseudogonatopus* spp. (dryinid wasps); *Metarhizium anisopliae* (fungal pathogen) |
| **Insecticide resistance** | BPH has high resistance development potential; use only when ETL exceeded |
| **Chemical** | Pymetrozine 50% WG @ 0.6g/L; Dinotefuran 20% SG; Buporofezin 25% SC; Flonicamid; Imidacloprid (resistance widespread, reduce use); avoid synthetic pyrethroids (cause resurgence) |
| **Timing** | Apply only when ETL surpassed; focus on base of plant (hoppers congregate there); use high volume spray (>500 L/ha) |

### White-Backed Planthopper (Sogatella furcifera) — WBPH
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Damage** | Similar to BPH but less severe; hopperburn possible |
| **Distinction** | White band across mesonotum; more migratory than BPH |
| **Management** | Similar to BPH; lower resistance risk; many chemicals effective |

## 12.4 Leafhoppers

### Green Leafhopper (Nephotettix virescens) — GLH
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Importance** | Vector of Rice Tungro Virus (most important viral disease) |
| **Damage** | Direct feeding (minor); primarily important as virus vector |
| **Favorable** | High N; abundant weed hosts; warm, humid |

**Management:**
| Method | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Monitoring** | Sweep net; 1 sweep/second; ETL: 10 GLH/sweep (lowlands) or 5 GLH/sweep (if tungro present) |
| **Resistant varieties** | GLH resistance genes (Grh1-Grh6) |
| **Chemical** | Imidacloprid; Thiamethoxam; Ethiprole; avoid pyrethroids |
| **Biological** | *Anagrus* spp. (egg parasitoid); spiders |
| **Cultural** | Staggered planting; remove weed hosts; synchronize planting |

## 12.5 Leaf Feeders

### Rice Leaf Folder (Cnaphalocrocis medinalis)
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Damage** | Larvae fold leaves longitudinally; feed on green tissue inside fold; leaves turn white (scorched appearance) |
| **Favorable** | Dense canopy; high humidity; high N |
| **Importance** | Increasing in intensity with high-yielding varieties |

**Management:**
- ETL: 1-2 damaged leaves/hill OR 20% leaf damage at vegetative stage
- Conserve natural enemies: *Trichogramma chilonis*; *Apanteles*; spiders
- Chemical: Chlorantraniliprole; Flubendiamide; Cartap hydrochloride
- Avoid excess N
- Biological: *Bacillus thuringiensis*; *Beauveria bassiana*

### Rice Hispa (Dicladispa armigera)
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Damage** | Adult scrapes green tissue between leaf veins; larvae mine leaves (blotch mines) |
| **Favorable** | Dense planting; high humidity; early season |
| **Importance** | Outbreaks in wet season in Asia |

**Management:**
- Light traps
- Clip and destroy affected leaves (reduce eggs)
- Chemical: Carbaryl; Chlorpyrifos; Spinosad

### Rice Caseworm (Nymphula depunctalis)
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Damage** | Larvae cut leaf pieces, make tubular cases; float on water; feed on leaves |
| **Favorable** | Stagnant water; shaded fields |
| **Management** | Drain field 2-3 days; Carbaryl |

### Rice Skipper / Rice Swarming Caterpillar (Spodoptera mauritia, Mythimna spp.)
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Damage** | Caterpillars cut seedlings at base; can strip fields overnight |
| **Management** | Flood fields briefly to drown larvae; *Bt*; Chlorantraniliprole; early detection critical |

## 12.6 Gall Midge (Orseolia oryzae)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Damage** | Maggot feeds at growing point → produces tubular gall (silver shoot) instead of leaf; no panicle develops from affected tiller |
| **Distribution** | South and Southeast Asia; Africa (*O. oryzivora*) |
| **Favorable** | High humidity; continuous flooding; continuous cropping |

**Management:**
- Resistant varieties most effective (gall midge biotypes exist)
- Remove and destroy affected tillers
- Crop rotation with non-host (pulse, oilseed)
- Chemical: Carbofuran 3G; Phorate; Fipronil
- Biological: *Platygaster oryzae* (parasitic wasp)

## 12.7 Rice Grain-Sucking Bugs

### Rice Bug / Gundhi Bug (Leptocorisa oratorius)
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Damage** | Adults and nymphs suck milk from developing grains → pecky rice, empty grains, discolored grains |
| **Favorable** | Late-planted fields; nearby weed hosts (*Imperata*, grasses) |
| **Yield loss** | 10-30% in endemic areas |

**Management:**
- Early planting
- Remove weed hosts
- Net sweeping
- Chemical: Imidacloprid; Thiamethoxam; Malathion; Lambda-cyhalothrin

## 12.8 Other Important Pests

| Pest | Damage | Management |
|------|--------|------------|
| **Rice thrips** (*Stenchaetothrips biformis*) | Leaf curling; silvery streaks; stunted seedlings | Apply seed treatment (Imidacloprid); drain field briefly |
| **Rice root weevil** | Larvae feed on roots; stunting | Drain field; Carbofuran |
| **Rice mealybug** | On stems, leaf sheaths; honeydew | Conserve natural enemies; Imidacloprid |
| **Rice ear-cutting caterpillar** (*Mythimna separata*) | Cuts panicle bases at grain filling | Flood field; chlorpyrifos |
| **Springtails** (*Sminthurus viridis*) | Seedling damage in DSR | Chemical seed treatment |
| **Termites** | DSR; cut roots at seedling stage | Seed treatment with chlorpyrifos |

# 13. INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT (IPM) FOR RICE

## 13.1 Principles of Rice IPM

Rice IPM aims to maintain pest populations below economic injury levels through an integrated approach:

1. **Grow a healthy crop** — Proper nutrition, water management, and variety selection
2. **Conserve natural enemies** — Avoid unnecessary insecticides
3. **Observe fields regularly** — Weekly scouting from planting to harvest
4. **Farmers as experts** — Train farmers to understand their fields

## 13.2 Natural Enemies in Rice Ecosystems

| Natural Enemy | Preys On | Conservation Strategy |
|--------------|----------|----------------------|
| **Spiders** (wolf spiders, jumping spiders, orb weavers) | BPH, WBPH, GLH, leaf folder | Provide refuge; avoid broad-spectrum insecticides |
| **Mirid bug** (*Cyrtorhinus lividipennis*) | BPH and GLH eggs (most important BPH predator) | Avoid early-season spraying; maintain humidity |
| **Ground beetles** (*Ophionea*, *Chlaenius* spp.) | Stem borer larvae; caterpillars | Mulch; reduce tillage |
| **Damselflies and dragonflies** | Adult stem borers, planthoppers | Provide perching sites (bamboo stakes) |
| **Coccinellid beetles** (ladybugs) | Aphids, planthoppers | Flowering plants on bunds |
| **Staphylinid rove beetles** | Maggots, larvae in soil | Reduce tillage |
| ***Trichogramma* wasps** | Stem borer eggs | Conservation; augmentative release |
| ***Tetrastichus* wasp** | Stem borer eggs | Conservation |
| ***Anagrus* wasp** | GLH eggs | Conservation |
| ***Platygaster* wasp** | Gall midge | Conservation |
| **Entomopathogenic fungi** (*Beauveria*, *Metarhizium*) | BPH, WBPH, stem borers | Avoid fungicides that kill beneficial fungi |

## 13.3 IPM Decision-Making Calendar

| Growth Stage (DAT) | Monitoring Focus | Key Action | Chemical Treatment (Only if ETL exceeded) |
|--------------------|-----------------|------------|---------------------------------------|
| **0-20 DAT** | Seedling establishment; early weeds | Pre-emergence herbicide; conserve spiders | — |
| **20-40 DAT** | Stem borer (dead hearts); leaf folder; BPH; weeds | 1st post-emergence herbicide if needed; manual weeding | Cartap; Chlorantraniliprole (stem borer) |
| **40-60 DAT** | BPH; stem borer; leaf folder; disease scouting | Drain field 7 days (max tillering); K application | Pymetrozine (BPH); Avoid: pyrethroids |
| **60-80 DAT** | Panicle initiation; BPH; blast | Drainage; blast fungicide if needed | BPH: Dinotefuran; Blast: Tricyclazole |
| **80-100 DAT** | Flowering; neck blast; rice bugs | Avoid chemical use during flowering! | — |
| **100-120 DAT** | Grain filling; rice bugs; rodents | Rodent control; drain for harvest | — |
| **Post-harvest** | Rodents; stubble management | Summer plowing; remove residue | — |

## 13.4 Economic Threshold Levels (ETL) for Major Rice Pests

| Pest | Growth Stage | ETL |
|------|-------------|-----|
| Yellow stem borer | Vegetative | 1 egg mass/m² OR 5% dead hearts |
| Yellow stem borer | Reproductive | 1 moth/m² OR 2 egg masses/m² |
| Striped stem borer | Vegetative | 1 egg mass/m² OR 5% dead hearts |
| BPH | Vegetative | 5-10 hoppers/hill |
| BPH | Reproductive | 10-20 hoppers/hill |
| WBPH | Vegetative | 10-20 hoppers/hill |
| GLH | Vegetative | 10 GLH/net sweep (+ Tungro: 5/sweep) |
| Leaf folder | Vegetative | 1-2 damaged leaves/hill OR 20% leaf damage |
| Rice hispa | Vegetative | 1 adult/hill OR 20% damaged leaves |
| Gall midge | Vegetative | 1% silver shoots |
| Rice bug | Grain filling | 5 bugs/10 sweeps OR 1 bug/m² at milky stage |
| Fall armyworm | Seedling | 1 larva/2 plants |
| Rice thrips | Seedling | 60/12 wet hand sweeps (nursery) |

## 13.5 Rodent Management

| Species | Damage | Management |
|---------|--------|------------|
| Rice field rat (*Rattus argentiventer*) | Cuts tillers; damages stored grain | Burrow fumigation (aluminum phosphide); zinc phosphide baiting; bromadiolone (for chronic); trap-barrier system (TBS) |
| Bandicoot rat (*Bandicota bengalensis*) | Extensive burrowing; stem cutting | Deep plowing; flooding burrows; natural predators (snakes, owls) — install owl perches (T-shaped posts) |

### Trap-Barrier System (TBS)
- Plastic fence barrier around 100 m² area
- 10-15 multi-catch traps at gaps
- Placed before planting; catches dispersing rodents
- Highly effective for community-level management

# 14. HARVESTING & POST-HARVEST

## 14.1 Harvest Timing

### Indicators of Maturity
| Indicator | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Grain color** | 80-85% of grains turn golden yellow/straw color |
| **Grain moisture** | 20-24% (optimal for harvesting) — NOT 14% (that's for storage) |
| **Panicle** | Drooping; grains hard |
| **Days after heading** | 25-35 days depending on variety and temperature |
| **Window of harvest** | Too early = higher broken % in milling; Too late = shattering losses + rodent/bird damage |

### Delayed Harvest Consequences
| Problem | Cause | Loss |
|---------|-------|------|
| Shattering | Over-mature grains fall off panicles | 5-15% yield loss |
| Rodent/bird damage | Extended exposure | 3-10% |
| Lodging | Dry, brittle stems break | 5-30% |
| Grain discoloration | Prolonged wetting | Quality downgrade |
| Cracking | Re-wetting of dry grain | Poor milling quality |

## 14.2 Harvesting Methods

| Method | Description | Labor | Grain Loss | Cost |
|--------|-------------|-------|------------|------|
| **Manual (sickle)** | Cut at 15-20 cm above ground; 3-5 days to harvest 1 ha | 40-60 person-days/ha | 1-3% | Low |
| **Manual + threshing frame** | Same + manual beating | 50-70 | 2-5% | Low |
| **Mechanical reaper** | Walk-behind or self-propelled; cuts only | 5-8 | 1-2% | Medium |
| **Combine harvester** | Cuts + threshes + cleans + bags in one pass | 2-3 | 2-5% | High (fastest) |
| **Mini-combine** | For small fields; same as full combine | 2-3 | 2-5% | Medium-high |

### Combine Harvester Considerations
- Requires proper field drainage before harvest
- Moisture optimum: 20-24% (too wet = clogging; too dry = grain cracking)
- Adjust fan speed and sieve setting to minimize grain loss
- Uniform ripening essential

## 14.3 Threshing

| Method | Capacity | Grain Damage | Requirements |
|--------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Manual beating on frame | 100-200 kg/day | Low | 5-10 people |
| Pedal thresher | 200-400 kg/day | Low | 1-2 people |
| Power thresher (axial flow) | 500-1000 kg/hour | Low-medium | 5-8 HP engine |
| Combine (integrated) | 2-5 t/hour | Medium | Complex machinery |
| Animal trampling | 200-300 kg/day | Low | 3-5 bullocks |

## 14.4 Drying

### Importance of Proper Drying
| Moisture Content | Condition |
|-----------------|-----------|
| >22% | Unsafe; spoilage within 48 hours |
| 18-22% | Short-term storage (2-4 days) |
| 14-18% | Medium-term storage (2-4 weeks) |
| 12-14% | Safe for 3-6 months |
| <14% | Safe for long-term (6-12+ months) |

### Drying Methods

| Method | Capacity | Drying Time | Quality | Cost |
|--------|----------|-------------|---------|------|
| **Sun drying (mat)** | Small (1-5 t) | 2-3 days | Fair-good; variable | Very low |
| **Sun drying (concrete floor)** | Medium (5-20 t) | 2-3 days | Fair; UV damage possible | Low |
| **Solar bubble dryer** | 1-5 t | 8-16 hours | Good; consistent | Medium |
| **Batch dryer** | 5-50 t | 6-12 hours | Good-excellent | Medium-high |
| **Continuous flow dryer** | 50-500 t/d | 2-4 hours | Excellent | High |
| **Re-circulating batch dryer** | 10-50 t | 4-8 hours | Excellent (lowest crack %) | High |
| **In-store dryer** | Storage structure integrated | 48-72 hours | Good | Medium |

### Sun Drying Best Practices
- Spread grain 2-5 cm thick
- Stir every 1-2 hours for uniform drying
- Cover grain during heavy sun (12-2 PM) to avoid cracking
- Collect into heaps overnight to prevent re-moistening
- Target: 14% moisture for storage (use moisture meter)

### Mechanical Drying Temperature Guide
| End Use | Maximum Air Temp | Recommended |
|---------|-----------------|-------------|
| Seed rice | 43°C | Do not exceed 43°C |
| Parboiled rice | 60°C | - |
| Milling rice | 54°C | 38-54°C; avoid above 54°C |

### The 3-Hour Rule
After threshing, paddy should begin drying within 3 hours:
- >22% moisture: Start drying immediately
- 3-6 hours delay: Acceptable if spread in thin layer
- >6 hours delay: Milling quality declines; discoloration begins

## 14.5 Storage

### Storage Types
| Structure | Capacity (tons) | Duration | Pros | Cons |
|-----------|----------------|----------|------|------|
| **Traditional bin** (bamboo/mud) | 1-5 | 3-6 months | Low cost | Rodent access; moisture |
| **Metal silo** (galvanized / steel) | 1-100 | 6-24 months | Airtight; rodent-proof | Cost; condensation |
| **Concrete bin** | 10-1000 | 12-36 months | Durable; large capacity | High cost; permanent |
| **Plastic/fabric hermetic bags** | 0.05-1 | 12-36 months | Very effective; portable | Small capacity; puncture risk |
| **IRRI Super Bag** (hermetic) | 1 | 12-18 months | Kills insects without chemicals | Must seal properly |
| **Hot air hermetic storage** | 1-50 | 24-36+ | Best quality preservation | High cost; complex |

### Storage Losses
| Loss Type | Cause | % Loss (6 months) | Prevention |
|-----------|-------|-------------------|------------|
| Insects | Weevils, borers, moths | 5-20% | Hermetic storage; fumigation |
| Rodents | Rats, mice | 2-10% | Proofing; baiting |
| Moisture | High humidity; leakage | 2-5% | Sealed storage; proper drying |
| Molds | Fungi (*Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*) | 1-5% | Dry to <14% moisture |

### Hermetic Storage Advantages
- Oxygen depletion (by grain respiration) kills insects naturally
- No chemical fumigants needed
- Preserves germination and milling quality
- Prevents re-wetting
- Most recommended for small-to-medium farmers

## 14.6 Parboiling (Traditional Process for Milling Quality Improvement)

| Step | Time | Temperature | Purpose |
|------|------|-------------|---------|
| 1. Soaking paddy in hot water | 8-12 hrs | 60-70°C | Gelatinize starch; soften hull |
| 2. Steaming | 20-30 min | 100°C | Complete gelatinization; kill microbes |
| 3. Drying | 1-3 days | Sun or mechanical | Reduce to 14% moisture |
| 4. Milling | Standard | — | Higher head rice recovery (70-80% vs 50-60%) |

### Benefits of Parboiling
- Head rice yield increases 10-20%
- Grain harder, less broken during milling
- Grains become more nutritious (B vitamins move from bran into endosperm)
- Grains less sticky when cooked
- Insects less likely to attack (harder grain)
- Taste preferred in many markets

### Drawbacks
- Color darkens (not preferred for white rice markets)
- Cooking time longer
- Requires equipment and fuel
- Disposal of soaking water (pollution issue)

# 16. RICE PHYSIOLOGY & STRESS MANAGEMENT

## 16.1 Abiotic Stresses

### Drought Stress

| Growth Stage | Critical Period | Effect | Mitigation |
|-------------|-----------------|--------|------------|
| Seedling | 0-10 DAS | Poor germination; stand failure | Direct seeding with good soil moisture; drought-tolerant varieties |
| Active tillering | 20-40 DAT | Reduced tillering; stunting | Supplementary irrigation; mulching |
| **Panicle initiation** | **55-65 DAT** (most sensitive) | Spikelet sterility; yield loss 30-60% | Ensure irrigation at PI; drought-tolerant varieties |
| **Flowering** | **85-95 DAT** (most sensitive) | Complete spikelet sterility if severe stress | Irrigation critical; avoid stress at this stage |
| Grain filling | 100-120 DAT | Reduced grain weight; chalky grains | Supplementary irrigation |

### Submergence/Flood Stress

| Type | Water Depth | Duration | Effect | Management |
|------|-------------|----------|--------|------------|
| **Transient flash flood** | Complete submergence | 4-10 days | Stunting; death if prolonged (>10 days) | SUB1 varieties; re-establish if >50% damaged |
| **Stagnant flooding** | 25-50 cm | 1-4 weeks | Reduced tillering; etiolation | Stagnant-flood-tolerant varieties |
| **Deepwater flooding** | 50-400 cm | 1-4 months | Stem elongation; few tillers | Deepwater varieties with elongation ability |

### Salinity Stress

| Salinity Level | ECe (dS/m) | Effect | Tolerant Varieties |
|---------------|-----------|--------|-------------------|
| Low | 2-4 | Slight yield reduction | Most varieties |
| Moderate | 4-6 | 25-50% yield loss | CSR 27, CSR 30, CSR 36, Lunishree, Pokkali |
| High | 6-8 | 50-75% yield loss | Very few tolerant varieties |
| Extreme | >8 | Near complete loss | Limited traditional varieties (Pokkali, Nona Bokra) |

**Salinity Management:**
- Flush fields with fresh water to leach salts
- Use salt-tolerant varieties
- Avoid stress at seedling stage (most sensitive)
- Gypsum application for sodic soils
- Raised beds for coastal saline areas
- Mulching to reduce evaporation

### Temperature Stress

| Stress | Threshold | Effect | Management |
|--------|-----------|--------|------------|
| **Low temperature** (seedling) | <15°C | Poor growth; yellowing | Cold-tolerant varieties (japonica type); raise water level |
| **Low temperature** (flowering) | <20°C for >1 hour | Spikelet sterility (male sterility) | Select varieties with cold tolerance; plant at optimal time |
| **High temperature** (flowering) | >38°C for >2 hours | Spikelet sterility | Plant early; use heat-tolerant varieties |
| **High temperature** (grain filling) | >35°C day | Chalky grain; poor quality | Early planting; avoid late-maturing varieties |

## 16.2 Physiological Disorders

| Disorder | Symptoms | Cause | Management |
|----------|----------|-------|------------|
| **Straight head** | Panicles erect; empty spikelets; chalky grains | Low temperature at PI; also associated with Zn deficiency | Balanced nutrition; planting at normal time; Zn application |
| **Panicle exsertion failure** | Panicle trapped in sheath | Boron deficiency; sheath rot disease; low temperature | Borax 0.5% foliar; disease control |
| **Chalkiness** | White, opaque areas in grains | High temperature during grain filling; excess N | Avoid late planting; moderate N; select varieties with low chalkiness |
| **Unfilled grains** | High % of sterile/hollow spikelets | Temperature stress; B deficiency; poor pollination | Genetic × environment interaction; manage specific factor |
| **Hollow grain / Grain discoloration** | Empty hulls; brown/black spots | False smut; grain rot; insect feeding | Disease management; grain-sucking bug control |

# 17. RICE CROPPING SYSTEMS & ROTATIONS

## 17.1 Major Rice-Based Cropping Systems

| System | Region | Description |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| **Rice-Wheat** | India (Indo-Gangetic Plains), Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, China | Most important in South Asia; rice in kharif (monsoon), wheat in rabi (winter); 13.5 million ha |
| **Rice-Rice** | South India, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Vietnam | Double rice cropping per year; boro (winter) + aus/aman (summer) in Bangladesh |
| **Rice-Pulse** | Central/East India (rice-blackgram, rice-lentil, rice-chickpea) | Nitrogen fixation benefit; break pest cycles |
| **Rice-Oilseed** (rice-mustard, rice-groundnut, rice-sesame) | India, Bangladesh | Mustard has deep taproot; breaks hardpan |
| **Rice-Maize** | Bangladesh, Philippines, Myanmar | Emerging system; crop diversification |
| **Rice-Vegetable** | Peri-urban areas | High-income; high management |
| **Rice-Fallow** | Rainfed uplands (Central India, Africa) | Low productivity; fallow period used for pasture/grazing |
| **Triple Rice** (Rice-Rice-Rice) | Vietnam (Mekong Delta), Philippines, China | Highly intensive; three crops/year; high pest pressure |
| **Rice-Aquaculture** | China, Vietnam, India (Kerala) | Fish/prawns with rice; integrated system |
| **Rice-Duck** | China, Japan, Korea, Bangladesh (traditional) | Ducks control weeds and pests; manure fertilizes rice |

## 17.2 Rotational Principles

| Rotation Benefit | Mechanism |
|-----------------|-----------|
| Pest cycle disruption | Soil-borne diseases (sheath blight, nematodes) decline during non-rice crop |
| Weed shift | Different crops favor different weeds; rotation keeps any one weed from dominating |
| Nutrient balance | Rice depletes K and Zn; pulses fix N; oilseeds break hardpan |
| Organic matter management | Rice straw returned; wheat/maize residue different quality |
| Water saving | Non-rice crops use less water; groundwater recharge during non-rice season |

### Recommended Rotation Period Between Rice Crops
- **Minimum**: 3-4 months fallow/non-rice crop
- **Optimal**: 6-12 months

## 18.3 Rice in the Indo-Gangetic Plains (Punjab-Haryana-Western UP)

### The Rice-Wheat System
- Dominant cropping system covering ~10 M ha
- **Challenges**: 
  - Groundwater depletion (Punjab: 3-5 ft/year drop)
  - Straw burning (air pollution crisis)
  - Herbicide-resistant weeds (*Phalaris minor* in wheat, *Echinochloa* in rice)
  - Pest resistance (BPH to imidacloprid)

### Conservation Agriculture Alternatives
| Practice | Adoption | Benefits |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Direct-Seeded Rice (DSR) | Increasing in Punjab | 25-40% water saving; labor saving; earlier maturity |
| Happy Seeder (direct drill in rice straw) | Supported by government | No burning; seeding wheat in standing rice straw |
| Laser Land Leveling | Widespread adoption | 20-30% water saving; uniform crop |
| Short-duration varieties (PR 126, PR 121) | Growing | 10-15 days shorter = less water |
| Delayed transplanting (after June 15) | Government regulation | Avoids peak evaporation; but risk of cold stress at heading |

## 18.4 System of Rice Intensification (SRI) in India

| State | SRI Adoption (ha) | Key Results |
|-------|-------------------|-------------|
| Tamil Nadu | >100,000 | 5-7 t/ha (vs 3-4 conventional); 40% water saving |
| Andhra Pradesh | >50,000 | 6-8 t/ha; promoted by government |
| Odisha | >50,000 | 4-6 t/ha in rainfed areas |
| Bihar | >30,000 | 4-6 t/ha with less input |

## 18.5 Rat Leakage Problem in Indian Rice Storage

Traditional storage structures (mud bins, bamboo bins) in India have rat leakage losses of 3-10%. Improved hermetic storage (metal bins, Super Bags) reduces this to <1%.
